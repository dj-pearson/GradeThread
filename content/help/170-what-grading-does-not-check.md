A certificate is a specific claim, and being clear about its edges is what
makes the claim worth anything. Three things it does not say.

## It does not check authenticity

The model assesses condition. It does not decide whether a bag is a real
Balenciaga or whether a jacket's label was sewn in last week.

These are genuinely different jobs. Authentication looks at construction
details, hardware stamps, stitch counts, serial formats and provenance, against
a reference set for one brand. Condition grading looks at wear, damage and
cleanliness, against a rubric that is the same for every garment.

A garment can be a perfect 9.5 and be a fake. A garment can be a genuine
archive piece at a 4. The two axes are independent, and a service that
pretended otherwise would be lying to somebody.

If authenticity is the question you have,
[condition grading vs authentication](/grading/vs-authentication) explains what
each actually establishes, and there is a separate
[authenticity check tool](/tools/authenticity-check) that is honest about being
a screen rather than a verdict.

## It is not a valuation

Condition is one of the biggest levers on what a garment sells for, and it is
the one you control. It is not the biggest.

Brand, size, current demand, season and how many other people are listing the
same thing this week all move price more than half a point of condition does.
A 9.0 in an unpopular size sells for less than a 7.0 in the size everyone
wants.

What the grade gives you is the ability to price the condition honestly instead
of guessing. The [Condition Index](/condition-index) shows the shape of the
price-versus-grade curve from real sold comps, and
[what's it worth](/whats-it-worth) applies it to a specific item.

## It cannot see what was not photographed

The grade assesses the garment as photographed. A stain on the inside of a
sleeve that appears in no photo is not in the grade.

This is not a loophole; it is the reason
[the photos we need](/help/grading/the-photos-we-need) is a required set rather
than a suggestion, and the reason a grade with low confidence is held rather
than published.

It also means hiding a flaw does not buy you anything. It lowers confidence,
which delays the grade, and then the buyer finds the flaw anyway and opens a
case. The certificate will not defend you, because it never claimed to cover
what it could not see.

## What it does claim

Given these photos, this garment is in this condition, assessed against a fixed
and published rubric, with this much confidence.

That is a narrower claim than "this is a great jacket" and a far more useful
one, because it is checkable. A buyer can open the certificate, see the same
photos the grade was made from, see the factor breakdown, and decide whether
they agree.

## And it says how it was made

Every certificate states that the assessment is produced by an automated system
from the seller's photos, and whether a human reviewer checked it before it
published.

That disclosure is not a disclaimer bolted on by a lawyer. It is part of what
makes the number trustworthy: a buyer who knows how the grade was produced can
judge how much to lean on it, and a service that obscured that would be asking
for trust it had not earned.

The full method, including how the model is evaluated and what its measured
error is against expert reviewers, is published at
[the methodology page](/grading/methodology) and the
[transparency report](/transparency).

## It is not a promise about the future

A grade describes the garment on the date it was assessed. It is not a warranty
that the garment will still be that way in six months, and it does not travel
with the garment through another owner's wardrobe on its own.

That is what the [garment passport](/scan) is for: a record of the grades a
garment has actually received over time, so a second-hand piece with two
assessments five years apart says something a single snapshot cannot.

## Where the limits are written down

None of this is buried. The [methodology page](/grading/methodology) states what
the model is trained on, how it is evaluated, and what happens when it is
unsure. The [transparency report](/transparency) publishes the measured
agreement with expert reviewers and the buyer dispute rate.

Publishing the error rate is unusual and it is deliberate. A service that only
tells you when it is right is a service you cannot calibrate against, and a
grade you cannot calibrate against is worth about as much as an adjective.
