A sale tells you what a buyer paid. A payout tells you what you received. The
gap between them is fees, postage and promotions, and reconciliation is where
that gap becomes visible.

## What it does

You import a payout report from the channel. FlipDesk matches each line to a
sale you already have, and attaches the real numbers: gross, fees, postage,
promotion cost, net.

Once matched, the item moves to Completed and you finally know what it made
rather than what it sold for.

## Why the two numbers differ

A 40 pound sale rarely puts 40 pounds in your account.

**Channel fees.** A percentage, sometimes with a fixed component, sometimes
varying by category.

**Postage you bought through the channel.** Deducted from the payout rather than
charged separately.

**Promotion.** If you ran promoted listings, the cut comes off here, and this is
where a promotion that felt cheap turns out not to have been.

**Adjustments.** Refunds, returns and disputes from earlier weeks land in
whichever payout happens to be next, which is why a payout can be smaller than
the sales in the same period.

The breakdown is per line, so a payout that looks wrong can be read rather than
guessed at.

## Unmatched rows

Some lines will not match, and that is information rather than a failure.

**A sale FlipDesk does not know about.** You sold something outside the app.
Match it by hand or create the item.

**An adjustment.** A refund or a fee correction with no sale of its own. Dismiss
it or record it as an expense.

**A name mismatch.** The channel's line refers to the item differently from your
title. Match it by hand once and the pattern usually holds.

Nothing is discarded silently. An unmatched row stays visible until you decide
what it is, which is the correct behaviour for a number that will end up in your
accounts.

## Why bother

Two reasons, and the second is the one people underrate.

**You learn what you actually made.** Revenue is not profit and fees are larger
than most sellers estimate. If you also recorded what you paid for the item, this
screen is the only place the full picture exists.

**You learn which things are worth selling.** A category that looks strong on
gross can be poor on net once fees and postage are counted. That is not visible
anywhere else, and it changes what you buy next, which is the decision with the
most leverage in the whole business.

## Doing it regularly

Once a week or once a payout cycle is enough. Reconciliation gets harder the
longer you leave it, because the adjustments accumulate and the memory of what
each row was fades.

If you are behind, work newest first. Recent rows are the ones you can still
identify, and old unmatched rows can often be dismissed in bulk once you accept
that you will not remember them.

## What comes out the other end

Completed items with real numbers, an expense record that ties to bank
statements, and a profit-and-loss view that is built from what happened rather
than from what you charged.

That is also what makes a tax return a report rather than an evening of
archaeology.

## Keep the evidence

Payout reports are the record behind your accounts, and channels do not keep
them forever.

Download and keep each one you import. FlipDesk holds what it matched, and the
original file is what settles a question about a line it did not.

That habit costs nothing during the year and saves an afternoon at the end of it.
