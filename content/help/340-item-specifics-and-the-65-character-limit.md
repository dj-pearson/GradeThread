Item specifics are the structured fields on an eBay listing: Brand, Size,
Colour, Material, Style, Department. They matter more than the description, and
they carry one rule that quietly breaks publishes.

## Why they matter more than the description

eBay's left-hand filters are built from item specifics, not from your title or
your body text.

A buyer who filters to Brand: Barbour, Size: L, Colour: Navy is looking at a
list your listing is either in or absent from. If Colour is blank, you are not
in it, regardless of the word "navy" appearing four times in your description.

That is the whole argument. Specifics decide which searches you appear in;
the description only persuades somebody who already found you.

## Required, recommended and optional

eBay marks specifics per category, and the category tree decides which.

**Required** ones block publishing. FlipDesk checks these before sending, so the
failure happens at your keyboard rather than as an error half an hour later.

**Recommended** ones do not block and do affect placement. Fill them.

**Optional** ones are worth filling when they are true and are not worth
inventing.

Because required specifics vary by category, changing an item's category changes
which fields you must supply. That is one of the reasons to catalogue the
category correctly early.

## The 65-character limit

Every item specific value must be 65 characters or fewer. This is eBay's rule
and it is enforced at publish time.

The failure mode is unpleasant. eBay can create the offer and then reject the
publish, which leaves the listing in a state that is neither draft nor live. It
shows as stuck rather than as an obvious error, and sellers who do not know
about the limit have historically spent a long time looking for a different
cause.

FlipDesk now flags any value over 65 characters in the composer as you type, so
the problem surfaces before you press publish. If you meet a stuck offer, the
length of a specific is the first thing to check.

The values that most often go over are Material on something with a complex
composition, and Style on a garment somebody has described lovingly. Shorten
them. The full text belongs in the description, where there is no such limit.

## Filling them without typing them

Most specifics come from what you already catalogued. Brand, size and colour map
straight across, so cataloguing well is what stops this being data entry twice.

The composer offers suggestions from eBay's own recommendations for the category
and from what has worked on similar items. Treat those as a draft and check them,
because a confidently wrong Material propagates into the filters buyers use.

## When eBay changes the requirements

eBay revises which specifics are required per category, sometimes with little
notice, and a listing that published fine last month can be rejected now.

Revising a live listing runs the same completeness check as publishing a new
one, so a gap that has appeared since is caught and filled rather than causing
the revision to fail. That check exists because it was missing once and revisions
were failing for a reason nobody could see.

## The short version

Fill everything required, fill everything recommended, keep each value under 65
characters, and put the poetry in the description.

## Fill them even when they feel obvious

"Department: Women" feels redundant when the title says so. It is not, because
the filter is built from the specific and not from the title.

The same goes for Colour, Size Type and Style. Each one is a filter somebody
uses, and being absent from a filter is indistinguishable from not existing.

It takes seconds when the values map from your catalogue, which is the argument
for cataloguing properly in the first place.
