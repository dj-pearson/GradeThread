A workspace lets more than one person work on the same inventory. This is how
to add people, what each role can do, and the one thing about ownership that is
worth understanding before you invite anybody.

## Sending an invitation

Account, then Team, then invite by email address and pick a role.

They get an email with a link. Following it either signs them in, if they
already have a GradeThread account on that address, or walks them through
creating one. Either way they land in your workspace rather than a new empty
one.

Invitations expire. If somebody sits on one for a fortnight, send a fresh one
rather than trying to work out why the old link errors.

## The roles

**Owner.** You. One per workspace. Owns billing, can remove anybody, and is the
only role that can transfer ownership or delete the workspace.

**Admin.** Everything an owner can do except billing and ownership. This is the
right role for a business partner or a manager who runs listings day to day.

**Member.** Does the work: adds items, grades, photographs, measures, drafts and
publishes listings. Cannot change billing, cannot invite or remove people,
cannot disconnect a marketplace.

**Viewer.** Reads. Sees inventory, grades and reports; changes nothing. Useful
for an accountant, or for anyone who needs the numbers and should not be able
to publish.

Give people the smallest role that lets them do their job. Not because anyone
is untrustworthy, but because a marketplace connection that can create and edit
live listings is a real permission, and it should be held by the people whose
job it is.

## What a member shares, and what they do not

They share the workspace: inventory, grades, listings, photos, the marketplace
connections, the grade allowance.

They do not share your login. Everyone signs in as themselves, which is the
whole point. Passing one password round means you cannot tell who published the
listing with the wrong price, and you cannot remove one person's access without
changing everybody's.

The audit log records who did what. That is not surveillance, it is what makes
"which of us relisted that" answerable in ten seconds.

## Billing

One subscription covers the workspace. Members do not need their own plans and
are not charged separately.

The grade allowance is shared. If your plan includes 100 grades a month and
three people are grading, that is 100 between you. Credits work the same way.

This surprises people who expected per-seat billing, and it is worth saying
plainly: adding a person does not increase your bill. It increases how fast you
get through the allowance you already have.

## Removing somebody

Team, find them, remove. Their access ends immediately, including any open
session.

Everything they made stays. Items, grades, listings, photos and measurements
belong to the workspace. This is the ownership point worth knowing before you
invite anybody: a member is doing work on your inventory, not on their own, and
nothing walks out with them.

Their name remains in the audit log for the actions they took, because a
history that quietly rewrites itself when someone leaves is not a history.

## Transferring the workspace

Only the owner can, and it is deliberate friction: it moves billing, the
marketplace connections and the ability to delete everything to somebody else.

If you are selling the business or handing it over, that is the mechanism. If
you just want somebody to run the day to day, make them an admin instead.

## A note on marketplace connections

The eBay connection belongs to the workspace, not to the person who made it.
That is what lets a member publish without each of them connecting their own
account, and it is why disconnecting is restricted to owners and admins.

It also means the connection outlives the person. If the member who originally
authorised eBay leaves, listings keep publishing, because nothing was tied to
their login. Only the workspace's authorisation matters.

The corollary is worth saying: anyone with member access can create and edit
live listings on your eBay account. That is the job, and it is also the reason
to give viewers the viewer role rather than making everybody a member for
convenience.
