Invoices are self-service. Refunds are a conversation, and this is how to make it
a short one.

## Invoices

Every charge produces one, and they are all in the billing area. Download them
individually; nothing needs requesting and nothing expires.

Set your business details before you accumulate a year of them. The details are
stamped onto the invoice at the time it is issued, so adding them later fixes
future invoices rather than past ones, and reissuing a year's worth
retrospectively is considerably more work than one form now.

## What the policy covers

The [refund policy](/refund) is the published position: what is refundable, in
what circumstances, and within what window.

Read it before asking rather than after. It is short, and it will usually tell
you the answer directly.

## What sits above the policy

If you are a consumer in the EU or the UK, you have statutory rights that a
published policy cannot reduce.

That is not a courtesy, it is how the law works: a term in a policy that
contradicts a statutory right is unenforceable, not binding. Where the two
disagree, the statutory right applies.

Practically: if you believe a statutory right applies, say so in your ticket and
say which. It changes how the request is handled and it saves a round trip.

## Asking for a refund

Open a ticket with three things:

**Which charge.** The invoice number or the date and amount. "The last one" is
ambiguous on an account with a subscription and a credit purchase in the same
week.

**What happened.** Charged twice, charged after cancelling, bought the wrong
thing, a feature did not work.

**What you want.** A refund, a credit, or the charge reversed and the plan
changed. These are different outcomes and guessing which you meant slows it down.

Most refund requests are answerable in one reply given those three. The ones that
take longer are usually the ones missing the first.

## The common cases

**Charged after cancelling.** Check the date. Cancelling stops the next renewal
and does not reverse the period you were already in. If the charge is genuinely
after the period ended, that is a mistake and it will be fixed.

**Charged twice.** Send both invoice numbers.

**Bought the wrong thing.** Say so. Buying a credit pack when you meant a
subscription is an ordinary mistake and is treated as one.

**A feature did not work.** This is a support question before it is a billing
one, and it is worth describing the failure rather than only the charge, because
the fix is often better than the refund.

## Credits and refunds

Credits are refundable under the same policy as anything else, and unspent
credits do not expire, so there is rarely a hurry.

What credits cannot do is convert to cash on request. They are a discount on
grading rather than a stored balance, which is a deliberate limit explained in
[Rewards and credits](/help/buyers/rewards-and-credits).

## Getting your details on the invoice

The billing details are stamped onto an invoice when it is issued. Setting them
now fixes every future invoice and none of the past ones.

If you are trading as a business, do it before the next renewal rather than at
the end of the year. Reissuing twelve invoices retrospectively is a request
somebody has to process by hand; entering the details once is a form.

## What we can see, and what we cannot

We can see that a charge was attempted, whether it succeeded, and the amount.

We cannot see why your bank declined something. A decline reaches us as a
decline, without a reason, which is why a card that is definitely fine and
definitely failing is a conversation with your bank rather than with us.

Saying that up front saves a round trip, because "have you asked your bank" is an
unsatisfying first reply to receive.
