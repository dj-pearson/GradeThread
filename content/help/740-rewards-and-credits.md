There are two separate things here, and they are easy to confuse. Credits are
spendable. Levels are not.

## Credits

A credit pays for one grade. You can buy them, and you can earn them.

**Referrals.** When somebody you referred signs up and qualifies, you get
credits. The qualifying step exists so that inviting people who never use the
product does not pay.

**Confirming grades.** Buyers who confirm what they actually received against a
certificate earn credits, because that confirmation is genuinely valuable: it is
how measured accuracy stops being a claim we make about ourselves.

**Sharing.** Certificates shared off-platform that bring somebody in can earn
credits.

Earned and bought credits are identical once they are on the account. Neither
expires.

## What credits cannot do

They cannot be cashed out, transferred or traded. They buy grades and nothing
else.

That is a deliberate limit rather than a stingy one. A balance that could be
converted to money is a stored value instrument, which is a regulated thing to
operate and would change what this product is. A discount on grading is a
discount on grading.

## Levels

Separate from credits, and cosmetic.

You gain a level from grading activity, and levels unlock badges, profile
flair, and certificate frames. There is a seasonal track that runs quarterly.

None of it affects your grades. A level 30 seller's 8.2 is the same 8.2 as a
level 1 seller's, because the rubric is fixed and the model does not know who
submitted the photos. That is not an oversight; it is the whole basis of the
grade meaning anything.

What levels do affect is how a profile reads to a buyer, which is a real but
different benefit.

## The leaderboard

Public and **opt-in**. It ranks top referrers.

Opt-in matters: nobody appears on a public list because they used the product.
You choose to be there, and you can leave.

## Referrals in practice

Your referral link is in the referrals area. The person who follows it gets an
account, and you get credits when they qualify.

The qualifying step is the honest part of the design. Paying on signup rewards
volume of invitations; paying on qualification rewards inviting people the
product actually suits. The second is the one worth having.

## Grade confirmations

The most useful thing a buyer can do here, and the least obvious.

You bought something with a certificate. It arrived. Confirming whether it
matched takes seconds, earns credits, and feeds the accuracy measurement that
[the transparency report](/transparency) publishes.

That report is only worth reading because the confirmations behind it are real.
A published error rate assembled from nothing would be marketing.

## Why the level track is deliberately cosmetic

It would be easy to make levels do something material: faster grading, priority
review, a discount.

None of that exists, and it should not. A grade whose speed or scrutiny depended
on the seller's status would be a grade that means different things for different
people, which is precisely the property the whole product is built to remove.

So levels change badges, flair and certificate frames. They change how a profile
looks and they do not change what a number means.

## Where to see it all

The rewards area shows level, seasonal progress and what unlocks next. The
referrals area shows your link and what it has earned. Credits appear in billing
alongside the plan allowance.

Three places because they are three different things, and merging them would
imply a relationship between status and spending power that deliberately does not
exist.
