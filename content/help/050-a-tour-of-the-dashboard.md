The sidebar has more in it than most people need, because it serves two
different jobs from one account: grading garments, and running a resale
operation. This is what each part is for, so you can ignore the half that is
not yours.

## Grading

**Overview** is the landing page: recent grades, what is in progress, and
whatever needs your attention.

**Snap to Value** is the fast path. Point your phone at a garment, get a read
on condition and rough resale value. It is for the charity shop aisle, when the
question is buy or walk away, not for producing a certificate.

**Submissions** is every garment you have graded, with its report and its
certificate. If you are only here to grade things, this and Overview are the
whole product.

**Rewards** tracks a level and a seasonal track that build from grading
activity. Cosmetic, and safely ignored.

## FlipDesk

This is the reseller side. It exists because listing a graded garment well takes
eleven steps and doing them across four browser tabs is how mistakes happen.

**Overview** and **Search** are the ways in. **Inventory** is the single surface
where items live, with table, grid, kanban and prep views behind tabs rather
than as separate pages.

**AutoLister** drafts listings in batches. **Scheduled drops** publishes them on
a timer. **Verified** is your public seller profile.

**Sourcing** answers what to buy and where. **Import** brings in items in bulk.

**Pricing** holds repricing rules, bulk pricing and suggestions. **Marketplaces**
is where eBay and the other channels connect. **Reconcile** matches payouts
against sales, which is the least glamorous and most valuable thing in the
section.

If you are not selling at volume, none of this is for you and skipping it costs
you nothing.

## Account

**Account** is the hub: profile, billing, team, referrals, settings. They are
tabs inside it rather than separate sidebar entries, because eight nav items
for things you touch twice a year is a worse sidebar.

**Developers** is API keys and the sandbox, shown only if your role can manage
them.

**Help** is the article you are reading, plus search across everything.

## The header

Top right has the search, the help menu behind the question mark, and your
account.

The help menu is worth knowing about: Help Center goes here, Common questions
goes to the short FAQ, System status shows whether something is actually broken
before you write a ticket about it.

## The question marks

Scattered through the app are small question-mark buttons next to headings.
They open the article for that exact screen in a panel beside your work, rather
than a new tab.

That is deliberate. The moment you need help is usually the moment you are
half-way through a form, and a new tab loses it.

## What to do first

If you are grading: New Submission, four good photos, read the report. That is
the product.

If you are reselling: connect eBay in Marketplaces before anything else. Most
of FlipDesk needs a channel to talk to, and connecting it first means the rest
of the tour makes sense as you go rather than in the abstract.

## Keyboard and search

The command palette opens with the usual shortcut and jumps to any page or any
item by name. It is faster than the sidebar once you know what things are
called, and it reaches routes that are deliberately not in the nav because they
only make sense from somewhere else, like a specific batch queue.

FlipDesk Search is a different thing: it searches your inventory, not the app.
Brand, SKU, title, status. If you have more than a hundred items it becomes the
main way you find anything.

## What the sidebar hides

Sections you cannot use do not appear. AutoLister is absent below the plan that
includes it, and Developers is absent for a role that cannot manage API keys.

This is a deliberate choice over greying things out. A disabled menu item is an
advertisement, and an advertisement in a tool you are trying to work in is
noise. If you want to know what a higher plan includes, the
[pricing page](/pricing) says so properly.

Some routes exist without a nav entry because they are only reachable in
context: a batch queue needs a batch, an item editor needs an item. They are not
hidden, they are just meaningless without the thing they operate on.
