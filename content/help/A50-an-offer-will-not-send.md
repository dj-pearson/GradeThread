Sending an offer to people watching your listing is one of the strongest
sell-through levers on eBay. When it does not work, there are two quite
different reasons and the messages deliberately distinguish them.

## "Reconnect required"

Your eBay authorisation has lapsed, or was granted without a permission this
feature needs.

**You can fix this.** Reconnect from Marketplaces. It takes a few seconds, your
listings stay live throughout, and only new operations wait.

This is the good version of the problem, and the message says so in those terms
rather than making you guess.

## "Feature unavailable"

The permission this needs is one eBay grants only to approved applications, and
the approval is not in place.

**You cannot fix this**, and neither can reconnecting. It is a restriction on our
side, not a problem with your account, and no amount of retrying changes it.

That distinction is the entire reason the two messages are different. Telling
somebody to reconnect when reconnecting cannot help wastes their time and makes
them think the problem is theirs.

## Why the messages are separate

Before they were, both cases produced the same dead end, and sellers who could
have fixed theirs in ten seconds waited indefinitely for something to change.

Now the connection records which situation it is in, and the message you see is
the one that applies. If you see the reconnect wording, reconnecting genuinely
helps.

## What to do meanwhile

Offers can be sent by hand from eBay's own interface. It is slower and it works,
and for a listing with a lot of watchers it is worth the few minutes.

The other levers are unaffected: a price drop, a promotion, or relisting all
work normally, and a price drop notifies watchers on its own.

## Other reasons an offer fails

**No eligible watchers.** Offers can only go to people who are actually watching,
and eBay has its own rules about who qualifies. Nobody eligible means nothing to
send.

**The listing is not eligible.** Some formats and categories are excluded by
eBay.

**Too soon after the last one.** There are limits on how often the same watchers
can be offered to.

All three come back as eBay's own message rather than a generic failure, which is
the same principle as everywhere else in the marketplace integration: their error
names the cause and ours would not.

## If it says something else entirely

Open a ticket with the item and the exact wording. The wording is what
distinguishes these cases, and it is the only part of a report that reliably
narrows them.

## What else moves a watched listing

If offers are unavailable, three levers still work and one of them notifies
watchers on its own.

**A price drop.** eBay notifies watchers when a watched item's price falls, so
this reaches the same people without needing the offer permission.

**A promotion.** Paid placement, and worth measuring rather than assuming: the
cost comes off the payout, which is where a promotion that felt cheap turns out
not to have been.

**Relisting.** On channels that weight recency, a genuinely new listing with
fresh photos can outperform an edit to a stale one.

## Reading the connection status

The Marketplaces page shows the connection state, and it distinguishes a healthy
connection from one needing a reconnect from one missing a permission entirely.

Checking there first is faster than working it out from a failure inside a
feature, because the connection page names the state directly.

## Why offers are worth chasing

A watcher has already decided they want the item and has not decided about the
price. That is the narrowest, warmest audience available anywhere in resale, and
an offer is the only tool that speaks to exactly them.

Which is why the two failure messages were separated in the first place: the
difference between a seller who reconnects in ten seconds and one who assumes the
feature is broken is a meaningful number of sales over a year.
