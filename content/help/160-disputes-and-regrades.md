Two different things can be wrong with a grade, and they have different fixes.
Picking the right one saves everybody time.

## Regrade: the garment changed

You washed out the mark, replaced the zip, depilled the jumper, or found the
missing button.

The original grade was correct about the garment it saw. What you want now is a
new assessment of a different garment, so you submit it again with fresh photos
and it grades from scratch.

This is worth doing more often than people do. Cosmetic appearance is 20
percent of the grade and a lot of what sits in it comes out in a wash. Half a
point of overall grade is frequently worth more than the twenty minutes.

The old grade and its certificate stay valid for what they were. They are a
record of the garment at that date, not a claim about it forever.

## Dispute: the grade was wrong

The garment has not changed and you think the score does not describe it.

Open the submission and dispute it. Say specifically what you disagree with,
and which factor. "This is a 7 not a 5" is harder to act on than "the fabric
score treats the surface as pilled, and that texture is the weave".

A human reviewer looks at the same photos and either confirms the grade or
adjusts it. If they adjust it, the certificate is revised and the new grade is
what the link shows.

Disputes are not held against the account. They are the mechanism by which
model accuracy is measured against expert judgement, which is what the
[transparency report](/transparency) publishes. A dispute that succeeds is
useful data; a dispute that fails tells you the photos said something you did
not intend them to.

## What happens to a shared certificate

A revised grade does not break the link.

The certificate URL you put in a listing keeps working and shows the current
grade, with a note that it was revised. It does not silently swap the number
and pretend nothing happened, because a buyer who screenshotted the old one
deserves to see that it changed rather than to think they misread it.

That matters more than it sounds. Certificates end up in buyers' order
histories and in dispute threads on marketplaces, and a link that dies or
quietly changes is worse for you than one that says plainly what happened.

## If you are the buyer

If you bought something on the strength of a certificate and the garment does
not match it, that is the
[buyer guarantee](/buyer-guarantee), not a dispute.

The guarantee covers "materially not as graded": the garment differs from the
certificate in a way that would have changed your decision. It has its own
claim process and mediation, and it is deliberately separate from a seller
disputing their own grade.

You can also report a certificate directly from its page. That routes to
moderation rather than to the seller.

## What is not a dispute

**The buyer disagrees with the grade.** That is the guarantee route.

**The photos were bad.** Resubmit with better photos. A reviewer looking at the
same unusable photos will reach the same conclusion the model did.

**The grade is held under review.** That has not been published yet, so there
is nothing to dispute. See
[Confidence and human review](/help/grading/confidence-and-human-review).

## How long it takes

A dispute goes to a person, so it takes longer than a grade does. You get a
notification when it resolves either way, including when the reviewer confirms
the original, because "we looked and stand by it" is an answer and silence is
not.
