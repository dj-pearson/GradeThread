The unglamorous stage. It is also the one that quietly decides how well your
next twenty listings do.

## Getting it out

The item sits at Sold with a next action of "ship". Buy the label through the
channel or however you normally do it, pack it, add the tracking number, and it
moves to Shipped.

Adding the tracking number in FlipDesk is worth the ten seconds even when the
channel already has it, because it is what ties the parcel to the item, the sale
and eventually the payout. Reconciliation later reads that chain.

## Why speed matters more than it looks

Dispatch time is measured on every major marketplace, and most of them use it as
a search-placement input.

That means a slow week does not cost you on the sale you were slow about. It
costs you on the listings you create afterwards, quietly, by placing them lower
than they would otherwise have been. It is the least visible penalty in resale
and one of the most expensive.

The practical version: ship the same day or the next working day, and if you
cannot for a stretch, use the channel's holiday setting rather than letting the
metric slide.

## Packing

Two failure modes are worth designing against.

**Damage in transit.** A garment does not need much, but anything with hardware,
a zip, buttons, a buckle, benefits from being folded so the hardware is inside
rather than pressed against the outside of the bag.

**Damp.** A polymailer left on a doorstep in the rain is how a perfectly graded
jacket arrives with a water mark. A cheap inner bag solves it.

Neither is exotic. Both turn into a not-as-described case that the certificate
cannot help you with, because the certificate describes the garment as it was
when you photographed it.

## Tracking is evidence

Tracking protects you from one specific and common claim: item not received.

Without tracking, that claim is effectively decided against you, because you
cannot show otherwise. With it, delivery is a fact somebody else recorded.

It is worth it on anything you would mind losing, which for most sellers is
almost everything. The exception is genuinely low-value items where the postage
saving beats the occasional loss, and that is a calculation, not a habit.

## International

Worth doing and worth being deliberate about. Customs paperwork, longer
delivery windows and a higher rate of item-not-received claims are all real, and
so is a considerably larger buyer pool for anything niche.

If you ship internationally, set the handling and delivery windows honestly on
the channel. A buyer who was told fourteen days and waits fourteen days is
content. The same buyer told four days is opening a case on day five.

## Then it waits for money

Shipped is not the end. The item stays there until the payout lands and
reconciles, at which point it becomes Completed.

That gap is usually days and occasionally weeks depending on the channel's
payout schedule. It is not a stall; it is the honest gap between a parcel
leaving and money arriving, and collapsing the two would mean counting revenue
you do not have.

[Reconciling payouts](/help/marketplaces/reconciling-payouts) is what closes it.

## Keep the packaging cheap and the parcel small

Postage is priced by size as much as weight on most services, and clothing
compresses.

A vacuum bag or a firm fold moves a coat down a price band, and the difference
compounds across a year of parcels. It is one of the few costs in resale you can
cut without cutting anything a buyer notices.

What a buyer does notice is a parcel that arrives clean and dry, which is a
different and much smaller expense.
