An item in FlipDesk has a status, and the statuses form a pipeline. Knowing the
shape of it is most of knowing how to use the product.

## The thirteen statuses

There are thirteen, and they are not all the same kind of thing.

**Steps you take:** sourced, cataloged, measured, photographed, graded, comped,
drafted, listed, sold, shipped.

**A state it waits in:** grading, which is the gap between sending photos and
the report coming back.

**Endings:** completed, when the sale is finished and reconciled, and returned,
when it comes back.

Most descriptions of this pipeline say eleven stages, counting the steps a
person performs and skipping the waiting state and the two endings. That count
is useful when explaining the workflow and confusing when you are looking at a
board with thirteen columns, which is why both are written down here.

## What each one means

**Sourced.** It exists. You bought it, or found it in a wardrobe. Usually
nothing but a name and a purchase price.

**Cataloged.** Brand, category, size, colour, SKU. The identity of the item, and
the fields every later step reads.

**Measured.** Chest, length, sleeve, waist. Optional, and the single biggest
lever on returns for anything fitted.

**Photographed.** The listing photos, which are a different set from the grading
photos and shot differently.

**Grading.** Sent, waiting. Usually under a minute; longer if the grade lands
below the confidence threshold and goes to a human reviewer.

**Graded.** The report is back. The item now has a number, a tier and a
certificate.

**Comped.** You have looked at what similar items actually sold for, so the
price is evidence rather than a guess.

**Drafted.** The listing exists in the composer: title, description, item
specifics, price.

**Listed.** It is live on at least one marketplace.

**Sold.** Somebody bought it.

**Shipped.** It is gone, with a tracking number.

**Completed.** The payout landed and reconciled against the sale.

**Returned.** It came back. Relist it or write it off.

## Where you are allowed to skip

Two stages are genuinely optional.

**Measurements** matter enormously for fitted clothing and barely at all for a
scarf. Skipping them on a low-value item is a reasonable trade; skipping them on
a blazer is how you end up paying return shipping.

**Comps** matter when you do not already know the price. If you have sold nine
of the same hoodie, you know what it goes for.

The two that nearly always repay the time are photographs and the grade. The
photographs are what somebody buys from. The grade is what stops the description
being your opinion, which is the whole reason the product exists.

## Moving items

The board view drags. Pick a card up, drop it in the next column, and the status
changes. Select several and move them together.

Backwards works too. An item that reached Drafted before its photos were right
can go back to Photographed, and nothing you already did is lost.

The board is not the only view. Inventory holds table, grid, kanban and prep
views behind tabs on one page, and the table is faster once you have more than
about fifty items.

## What the next action tells you

Every status carries a next action, and the card shows it. Sourced says "catalog
basic info". Photographed says "send to GradeThread". Sold says "ship".

That is the whole navigation model. You do not have to remember the order,
because the card says what it is waiting for. The pipeline view then shows how
many items are sitting at each stage, and the biggest column is usually where
your afternoon should go. A large Sourced pile is money you have already spent
and not yet listed.
