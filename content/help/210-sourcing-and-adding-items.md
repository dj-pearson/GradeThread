Everything in FlipDesk starts as an item. This is how items get in, and what to
fill in while you are there.

## The four ways in

**One at a time.** Add item, type a name, done. It lands as Sourced with
whatever you gave it. The right route when you are unpacking one thing.

**From a photo.** Snap it, and the details are read off the picture: brand,
category, likely size. Faster than typing, and everything stays editable
afterwards, which it will need to be.

**In bulk.** Import brings in a spreadsheet or a folder of photos and creates an
item per row or per photo. This is the route for a wholesale lot, and ten
minutes of column mapping beats an hour of typing.

**From the extension.** On a marketplace listing page, the browser extension can
appraise an item and add it. Useful when you are sourcing from another platform
rather than from a rail.

## Record what you paid

The cost field is the one people skip, and it is the one that makes every later
number mean anything.

Without it, reconciliation can tell you what you were paid. With it, the same
screen tells you what you made, which is a different and considerably more
useful number. There is no way to reconstruct a cost afterwards except by
remembering, and in three weeks you will not.

If you bought a lot of forty things for one price, split it. Evenly is fine.
Allocating by expected value is better and takes longer. Either beats recording
nothing at all.

## Sources

A source is where a batch came from: a specific car boot sale, a wholesale
supplier, a house clearance, one particular charity shop.

Tagging items with a source is optional, and it answers a question nothing else
can: which of the places you go is actually worth going to. Two sources with the
same average sale price can have very different margins once you count what you
paid and how long the drive was.

You create sources in the Sources area and pick one when adding an item. Half an
hour of tagging pays for itself the first time you decide to stop going
somewhere.

## What to fill in now, and what to leave

Fill in now: a name you will recognise, the cost, and the source if you use
them.

Leave for cataloguing: brand, size, colour, condition notes, SKU. Those belong
to the next stage, and there is a screen designed for entering them quickly
across many items at once. Doing them one at a time while unpacking is slower
and more error-prone.

The exception is anything you will forget. If a garment has a story that affects
its value, a label detail, a provenance, a flaw you spotted in the shop, write
it in the notes now. In three weeks it will be one of forty black jackets.

## Naming things you can find again

Give items names you would search for. "Black Barbour waxed jacket L" beats
"jacket 3", and FlipDesk Search is how you will find it once there are two
hundred.

SKUs are optional and worth it above about fifty items. They are what tie a
physical thing on a shelf to a row on a screen, and the moment you cannot find
the garment somebody just bought, you will wish you had them.

## Then it waits

A newly added item sits at Sourced with a next action of "catalog basic info".
It will sit there indefinitely without complaining, which is convenient and is
also why the pipeline view puts the biggest column in front of you.

## The pile is the metric

The number worth watching is how many items sit at Sourced. Every one is money
you have already spent and have not yet offered to anybody.

Sellers who track anything usually track sales. Tracking the Sourced column is
more actionable, because it is the only number in the pipeline you can fix this
afternoon without anybody else's cooperation.
