Cards decline for boring reasons far more often than interesting ones. Here is
what happens and what to check.

## What happens first

You get an email, and a banner appears in the app.

The account keeps working through a grace period rather than dropping to free
immediately. That is deliberate: a card that expired on a Friday should not cost
somebody their weekend of listing, and the overwhelming majority of failed
payments are resolved within a day by updating a card.

Nothing is deleted at any point in this process.

## The three usual causes

**The card expired.** By far the most common. The number is unchanged and the
expiry date on file is last month's.

**The bank declined it.** Often a fraud check on a recurring international charge
rather than anything to do with funds. Banks are cautious about subscriptions
they have not seen before, and the fix is usually one call or one tap in a
banking app.

**Insufficient funds.** Self-explanatory, and worth ruling out before ringing
anybody.

Between them these account for nearly every failure. Something genuinely unusual
is rare enough that it is worth checking the boring three first.

## Fixing it

Update the card in the billing area. That triggers an immediate retry rather than
waiting for the next scheduled one, so a working card resolves it in seconds.

If the card is correct and it still declines, the next step is your bank rather
than us. A decline is the bank's decision and we receive only that it happened,
not why.

## The retry schedule

Failed payments are retried automatically over several days.

Updating the card is faster and ends the retries. Doing nothing means the retries
run their course, and if none succeeds the subscription lapses to free at the end
of the grace period.

## If it lapses

The same as cancelling: items, grades, certificates and credits all stay, and the
FlipDesk tooling and the larger allowance stop.

Resubscribe with a working card and everything reconnects, including your
marketplace connections. Nothing needs setting up again.

## Preventing it

Two habits.

**Update the card before it expires** rather than after. The expiry date is on
the card and on the billing page.

**Use a card you will not cancel.** A card replaced after a fraud alert is a
common cause of a subscription lapsing months later, because the new number was
never added anywhere.

## If you think the charge was wrong

A failed payment and a disputed payment are different situations. If the charge
should not have been attempted at all, that is a billing question, and
[Refunds and invoices](/help/billing/refunds-and-invoices) covers what to send.

Raising a chargeback with your bank rather than a ticket with us is slower for
you and generally ends in the same place, so a ticket first is worth the ten
minutes.

## What the grace period is for

It exists because the overwhelming majority of failed payments are an expired
card and are fixed within a day.

Cutting access on the first decline would punish that case, which is nearly all
of them, in order to handle the rare one faster. The trade is deliberate and it
runs the way that suits an ordinary person having an ordinary week.

Nothing is deleted at any point, before or after the grace period ends.

## If you are mid-way through something

A failed payment does not interrupt work in progress. A batch keeps running, a
publish keeps publishing, and items keep their photos, measurements and drafts.

The banner is a notification rather than a block, and that is the intended
behaviour: discovering a card problem should not also mean losing an afternoon of
listing.
