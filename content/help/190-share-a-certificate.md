A certificate is a public page. Once a grade lands, the page exists and anyone
with the link, the number or the QR can open it without an account.

## The three handles on one certificate

**The link.** `gradethread.com/cert/<id>`. Open your grade and it is the page
you are looking at.

**The number.** A short code in the shape `GT-XXXXXXX`, printed on the
certificate itself. It is the handle to use when a link is not allowed or not
convenient. A buyer types it at [the verify page](/verify) and gets the same
page.

**The QR code.** At the foot of the certificate, and burned into the graded
photo. A phone camera resolves it straight to the page.

All three land in the same place. Which one you reach for depends on where you
are putting it.

## Sharing it directly

The share row sits near the top of the certificate.

**Share** opens your phone's share sheet where the device supports one, and
falls back to copying the link everywhere else. One-tap buttons for the usual
channels sit alongside it, and each of those unfurls the graded photo, so the
post shows the garment and the grade rather than a bare URL.

**Print** produces a clean copy with the interactive parts dropped. Choose "Save
as PDF" in the print dialog if you want a file to attach to an email.

If you are signed in, your referral code is baked into every link you share.
Someone who signs up after following it is attributed to you. Sharing is
recorded when you press the button, but the reward is paid on verified
click-throughs, which happen somewhere you do not control. That is deliberate.

## Putting it near the top of a listing

This is the part people get wrong, so it is worth being exact.

**Put the grade in the description text, high up.** The first two lines are what
a buyer reads before deciding to scroll. A line naming the grade and the
certificate number belongs there, not below your measurements and your returns
policy.

**Do not put a link in a marketplace description.** eBay treats an off-site URL,
or even a bare domain, as an offer to trade outside eBay, and the observed
outcome is a hidden listing rather than a warning. Publishing through FlipDesk
strips links out of descriptions for you, including out of drafts written before
the rule existed. The certificate number replaces the link and costs the buyer
about four seconds.

**Let the item specific carry it too.** Publishing adds a structured
`Condition Grade` specific with the value `GradeThread X.X`. It rides in the
same place a buyer looks for size and material, and it is a field marketplaces
can filter on.

**Nothing goes in the photo gallery.** Not an overlay on your own photo, and not
a separate generated grade card. See
[Embed a grade badge](/help/certificates/embed-a-grade-badge) for the reasoning
and for the three formats that are safe.

## What the buyer actually sees

Worth knowing, because it shapes how much you need to write yourself:

- The score out of 10.0 and the tier name.
- All five factors, each scored, each with its published weight. This is where a
  careful buyer looks, because it says where the wear is.
- The photos the assessment was made from.
- A plain statement that the assessment is automated, produced from your photos,
  and whether a person reviewed it before it published.
- An integrity check confirming the record has not been altered since it was
  issued.
- A route to file a buyer-guarantee claim if what arrives is materially not as
  graded.

You do not need to restate any of that in your listing. Name the grade, name the
number, and let the page do the rest.

## A screenshot is not a share

A screenshot of a certificate is an image of a claim. It cannot be checked, it
can belong to a different garment, and an experienced buyer discounts it
accordingly. Send the number or the link. Both take seconds and both are
verifiable, which is the entire point of having one.
