Grading is the step that makes a FlipDesk listing different from any other
listing. This is what happens between pressing send and the number arriving.

## Sending it

From an item at Photographed, the next action is "send to GradeThread".

It uses the grading photo set, not the listing photos. If you have not shot
them, this is the moment: flat, evenly lit, front, back, care label and at least
one detail. [The photos we need](/help/grading/the-photos-we-need) is the
checklist and it is worth two minutes here rather than a regrade later.

The item moves to Grading, which is a waiting state rather than a step. You can
leave the page; nothing needs you.

## What you are waiting for

Usually under a minute.

Longer if the grade lands below the confidence threshold, in which case it is
held for a human reviewer instead of published. That is the system working: a
low-confidence grade published immediately looks exactly like a confident one
and is wrong more often. See
[Confidence and human review](/help/grading/confidence-and-human-review).

Most held grades are held because of the photos rather than because the garment
was genuinely ambiguous, so if yours is waiting, the report will usually name
what it was unsure about.

## What arrives

The item moves to Graded and gains four things:

A **score** on the 1.0 to 10.0 scale, to one decimal place.

A **tier** name: NWT, NWOT, Excellent, Very Good, Good, Fair or Poor.

A **factor breakdown** across fabric, structure, cosmetic, function and odour,
with the published weights.

A **certificate** at its own public URL, which anybody can open without an
account.

## How it reaches the listing

Three ways, and they are automatic rather than something you assemble.

**The condition value.** Each marketplace has its own condition dropdown, and
the grade maps onto the right one so you are not translating by hand. See
[Condition mapping](/help/marketplaces/condition-mapping).

**The description.** The composer offers the report's written condition summary
as listing copy. It is already phrased as an honest, specific account of the
condition, which is the part most sellers find hardest to write.

**The certificate link.** Put it near the top of the description, not at the
bottom. The buyer who clicks it is the buyer who was hesitating.

The grade appears as text on the listing rather than as a badge burned into a
photo. That is deliberate: several marketplaces treat overlaid graphics on
listing images as a policy problem, and a listing removed for a watermark helps
nobody.

## If you disagree with the grade

If the garment has changed since, because you cleaned it or repaired something,
submit it again. That is a regrade and it assesses the garment you now have.

If it has not changed and you think the score is wrong, dispute it. A human
reviewer looks at the same photos. Both routes are in
[Disputes and regrades](/help/grading/disputes-and-regrades).

## Skipping the grade

Nothing stops you. The item moves on and lists like anything else.

What you give up is the reason a buyer chooses your listing over the identical
one next to it at the same price. The whole argument for grading is that it
converts your description from an assertion into something checkable, and a
listing without the certificate is back to being an assertion.

## The grade travels with the item

A grade attaches to the item, not to a listing. Relist it, cross-post it, or
have it returned and relisted, and the grade and its certificate follow.

That matters for returns particularly. An item that comes back does not need
regrading unless something changed in transit, and the certificate the original
buyer saw is the same one the next buyer sees.

If something did change in transit, regrade it. That is the honest move and it
takes a minute.
