Cancelling stops the subscription renewing. It is one action in the billing area
and there is no retention maze in front of it.

## What it stops

The renewal, at the end of the period you have already paid for.

You keep the plan and everything in it until then, because you bought it. Then
the account drops to the free plan: three grades a month, and no FlipDesk
tooling.

## What it does not touch

**Your items.** Every garment, with its photos, measurements and notes.

**Your grades.** Every report you have ever run.

**Your certificates.** Every one, still live at its URL.

**Your credits.** They do not belong to a plan and do not expire. If you come
back in a year, they will be there.

**Your live listings.** Cancelling does not end anything on a marketplace.

## Why certificates keep working

This is the part worth being emphatic about, because it is the part people
assume goes wrong.

A certificate link lives in a buyer's order history, in a marketplace message
thread, and sometimes in a dispute record. Those people did not cancel anything
and did not agree to anything.

Breaking the link would punish them for a decision they had no part in, and it
would also make every certificate anybody ever shared conditional on the seller's
future subscription. A trust signal that quietly dies when somebody stops paying
is not a trust signal, so it does not die.

## What you lose

The allowance beyond three grades a month, and the FlipDesk tooling: the
AutoLister, crosslisting, comps, repricing, reconciliation.

The reconciliation point catches people out. Historical figures stay readable,
but new payout imports need the plan. If you are cancelling mid-year, export what
you need for your accounts first.

## Coming back

Resubscribe and everything reconnects. Items, grades, marketplace connections
and credits are where you left them.

There is no penalty for having been away and no re-onboarding to sit through.

## Refunds

Cancelling is not a refund; it stops the next charge. Whether a past charge is
refundable is a separate question and depends on the
[refund policy](/refund) and, in the EU and UK, on statutory consumer rights
that the policy cannot shorten.

If you think you have been charged for something you should not have been, open
a ticket rather than cancelling and hoping. Cancelling does not reverse a charge
and does not start a refund.

## Deleting the account entirely

Different from cancelling, and it is in the account settings rather than
billing.

Deletion removes your data and is not reversible. Cancelling is the reversible
one, which is why they are separate actions in separate places.

## Before you cancel, export

Two things are worth pulling out first.

**Your payout and expense figures**, if you use reconciliation. Historical
numbers stay readable, but new payout imports need the plan, so a mid-year
cancellation means the rest of the year has to be assembled elsewhere.

**Your invoices**, if you claim them against tax. They stay available, but
downloading them while you are already in the billing area takes a minute and
saves finding your way back later.

## What happens to team members

They keep access to the workspace, at the free plan's capabilities.

Nobody is removed and nothing they made is affected. What stops is the tooling,
for all of you at once, because the plan belongs to the workspace rather than to
a person.

## Pausing instead

There is no pause, and for most seasonal sellers cancelling is the closest
equivalent.

Cancel at the end of a busy season, keep whatever credits you have, and
resubscribe when volume returns. Credits do not expire, the account keeps
everything, and the reconnection is immediate. That works out considerably
cheaper than holding a subscription through four quiet months, and it is the
pattern worth adopting deliberately rather than arriving at by accident.

The one cost is the FlipDesk tooling while you are away, so do the reconciliation
for the season you have just finished before you cancel rather than after.
