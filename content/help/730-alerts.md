An alert tells you when something matching your criteria appears with a
certificate. The useful part is not the brand filter, which every marketplace
has. It is the condition floor, which none of them do.

## Setting one up

From the buyer area. Give it a brand, a category, a size, a price ceiling, and a
minimum grade.

The minimum grade is the point. "Barbour, L, under 120, graded 8.0 or better" is
a search no marketplace can run, because a marketplace's condition field is four
words a seller chose.

## What it watches

Items with **public GradeThread certificates**.

That is a real limitation and worth stating plainly: it is a smaller pool than
everything listed everywhere. What you give up in breadth you get back in the
one thing that matters, which is that every item in the pool has an objective
condition assessment rather than a seller's adjective.

An alert is therefore a complement to your ordinary saved searches, not a
replacement for them. Keep both.

## Choosing a floor

A practical guide, using the anchors on the scale:

**8.0 and above** is Excellent or better: gently used with no notable flaws. Set
this when you want something that reads as nearly new.

**7.0 and above** is Very Good or better: light, even wear that does not affect
how it looks or works. This is the sensible default for most people and most
garments.

**6.0 and above** is Good or better: visible wear, entirely wearable. Right for
workwear, for anything you will wear hard, and for hunting bargains.

Setting the floor too high is the commonest mistake. It is quiet, so it feels
like nothing is out there, when in fact you asked for the top few percent of a
small pool.

## Reading an alert

Every alert links to the certificate, not just to the listing.

Open it. The factor breakdown is where the decision is: two items both above your
floor can differ in where their wear is, and fabric wear is permanent while a
broken zip is twenty minutes.

## Managing them

Two or three specific alerts beat one broad one. "Any coat over 7.0" fires
constantly and gets muted; "Barbour, L, over 7.5, under 150" fires rarely and
gets read.

Pause rather than delete when you stop looking for something. A paused alert
keeps its criteria, and rebuilding a good one from memory is annoying.

## What it cannot do

**It cannot see ungraded listings.** No certificate, no alert.

**It cannot tell you an item is genuine.** Nothing here does.

**It cannot promise the item is still available.** Alerts fire on appearance; a
good item at a good price can go in minutes, which is the argument for reading
them promptly rather than in a weekly digest.

## Alerts and the extension together

The two buyer tools solve adjacent problems and are better used together.

An alert tells you when something graded appears. The browser extension gives you
a condition read on the vast majority of listings that have no certificate at
all.

Between them: alerts cover the graded pool properly, and the extension covers
everything else at lower confidence. Relying on either alone leaves an obvious
gap.

## Tuning after a fortnight

Set an alert, leave it two weeks, then look at what it actually sent.

Nothing at all usually means the floor is too high or the price ceiling too low
for the pool. A flood means it is too broad to read, and a flood that goes unread
is the same as no alert.

Adjust one criterion at a time. Changing three at once and getting a different
volume tells you nothing about which change did it.
