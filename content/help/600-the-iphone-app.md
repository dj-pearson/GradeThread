The iPhone app exists for one reason above all others: photographs. Everything
else it does is a convenience, and this is the part that genuinely belongs on a
phone.

## Why a phone is the right tool here

Grading needs good photographs of a garment laid flat. The camera you have is
the one in your pocket, and it is better than most people's other options.

Without an app, the workflow is: photograph on the phone, transfer to a computer,
upload from the computer. That transfer step is where people stop. With the app
it is photograph, submit, done, and the report arrives on the same device.

## Signing in

Sign in with Apple is the smoothest route and the one most people should take.
Email and password works too, and both reach the same account.

Whichever you pick, use the same identity every time. Signing up with a password
and later using Sign in with Apple on the same email address joins them up
rather than splitting them, but it is simpler not to test that.

## What it does

**Capture.** Guided photo capture for grading, with the required set as prompts
rather than as something to remember.

**Submit and read.** Send for grading, get the report on the phone.

**Your submissions.** Everything you have graded, with certificates.

**Share into it.** Photos from any other app can be sent to GradeThread through
the share sheet. See
[Sharing into GradeThread](/help/mobile/sharing-into-gradethread).

**Notifications.** A push when a grade lands, so you are not checking.

## What still belongs on a desktop

Writing listings. FlipDesk's composer, the AutoLister and the reconciliation
screens are dense, and a phone is the wrong shape for a table of forty items.

The natural split is: photograph and grade on the phone, list and reconcile at a
desk. Both work on the same data, so items photographed on the phone are waiting
on the desktop.

## Staying signed in

The app keeps you signed in across launches. If it logs you out repeatedly, that
is a real problem rather than normal behaviour, and it is worth a ticket with
roughly how often it happens.

The interval matters when reporting it: an hourly logout and a daily one have
different causes, and saying which turns a long investigation into a short one.
See [Staying signed in](/help/mobile/staying-signed-in).

## Offline

Photographs taken while offline are held and uploaded when a connection returns.
Grading itself needs the network, because the assessment happens on a server.

That is worth knowing if you source somewhere with poor signal: you can capture
everything at the fair and let it upload on the way home.

## Getting it

From the App Store. Same account as the web, so nothing needs setting up twice.

## Notifications

The app can send a push when a grade lands. It is worth allowing, because the
alternative is opening the app to check, which people do far more often than the
grade takes.

The notification carries the item and the result rather than making you open it
to find out whether it was worth opening.

Declining costs you only that. Everything else works, and you can change your
mind in the phone's own settings later.

## Widgets

There is a home screen widget showing recent activity. It is a convenience rather
than a feature, and it is the sort of thing worth adding once you are grading
regularly enough that the number changes between glances.

## When to use the web instead

Anything involving many items at once. The dense FlipDesk tables, the AutoLister
queue, reconciliation, the composer's item specifics.

Those are not missing from the phone by oversight. They are screens that need
width, and a phone-shaped version of a forty-row table is worse than no version.
The workflow assumes you will photograph on a phone and list at a desk, and both
halves read the same data.
