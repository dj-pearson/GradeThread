A grade is a report on one garment: a score from 1.0 to 10.0, a breakdown
across five weighted factors, a written condition summary, and a certificate
you can share with a buyer. This is how to get a good one on the first try.

## Lay the garment out first

Do this before you open the app. It is the step that decides the quality of
everything after it.

Put the garment flat on a plain surface with nothing else in the frame. A bed
with a patterned duvet is a worse background than a bare floor. Smooth the
fabric so the shape reads clearly and no fold hides a seam or a hem.

Natural light, near a window, on an overcast day, is better than any lamp you
own. Direct sun creates hard shadows that read as marks, and a warm indoor bulb
shifts the colour enough that a white shirt grades as cream.

## The four photos

**Front.** The whole garment, filling the frame, shot straight down. Not at an
angle, and not from across the room. If the garment does not fit in one frame
flat, fold it once and say so, but a full flat shot is always better.

**Back.** Same again, turned over. The back is where wear on a collar, a seat
or a shoulder line shows up, and it is the one people skip.

**Care label.** Close enough to read. This is how the fabric composition and
the size are picked up, and both feed the grade. A blurry label costs you
accuracy in the fabric factor, which is the heaviest of the five at 30 percent.

**At least one detail.** A cuff, a hem, an armpit, a zip. If the garment has a
flaw, photograph the flaw. Hiding it does not raise the grade; it lowers the
confidence, and a low-confidence grade gets sent for human review rather than
published.

More detail shots help. More full-length shots do not: the second and third
front photo tell the model nothing the first did not.

## Submit

Open New Submission, add the photos, and fill in what you know: brand, garment
type, size. None of it is guessed at when you supply it, and every field you
leave blank is one the model has to infer from the pictures.

Then submit. Most grades come back in well under a minute.

## What comes back

The report leads with one number, the overall grade, rounded to one decimal
place. Under it are the five factors, each scored in half-point steps:

- Fabric condition, weighted 30 percent
- Structural integrity, 25 percent
- Cosmetic appearance, 20 percent
- Functional elements, 15 percent
- Odour and cleanliness, 10 percent

That weighting is fixed and published. A pilled jumper loses more than a
garment with one loose button, because fabric outranks function by two to one.

There is also a tier name: NWT at 10, NWOT at 9, Excellent at 8, Very Good at
7, Good at 6, Fair at 5, Poor at 3 to 4. The tier is what a buyer skims; the
number is what a buyer compares.
[What a grade means](/help/getting-started/what-a-grade-means) goes through
both.

## If the grade is not what you expected

First check the photos. Nine times in ten, a surprising grade is a photo
problem rather than a model problem, and the report will usually say which
factor it was unsure about.

If you still disagree, you can dispute it. That is not a complaint form; it
routes to a human who looks at the same photos and either confirms the grade or
changes it. See
[Disputes and regrades](/help/grading/disputes-and-regrades).

## Then share it

Every grade comes with a certificate at its own web address. Put that link in
your listing. A buyer can open it without an account and see the score, the
factor breakdown and the photos it was based on, which is the whole point: your
description stops being your opinion.
