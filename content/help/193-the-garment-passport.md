A certificate is one assessment on one day. A passport is the whole record of a
garment over time: every grade, listing, sale and ownership change, in order,
with a confidence mark on each.

## Where one comes from

You do not create a passport. Grading an item creates one, and the passport link
appears on the grade as soon as it lands. Grades from before the feature existed
may not have one, in which case the panel offers to start a passport by grading
the item.

The public page lives at `gradethread.com/passport/<slug>`. The slug is the only
identifier on it. Anyone can open it without an account, which is what makes it
useful to send to a buyer.

## What is on the page

**The garment**, named and pictured.

**The timeline**, oldest first: graded, listed, sold, ownership transferred, and
a few others. Grade events carry the score, the tier and the certificate, so you
can open any assessment in the chain and read it in full.

**A confidence mark on every event.** This is the part that makes a passport
different from a list of claims. An event is marked as established by a
first-party fact, as a probable match, or as unknown. A sale recorded through a
connected marketplace account is not the same kind of fact as a match inferred
from a photograph, and the page says which it is rather than flattening both
into "history".

**A chain strength summary**, which is how much of the record rests on
first-party facts rather than inference. A short, fully established chain is
worth more than a long, mostly inferred one, and this is where you see the
difference.

**No personal data.** Participants appear pseudonymously. A seller who has opted
into a verified public identity shows as that handle, and that is the only case
where a passport names anybody.

## Claiming one you received

Two routes in, depending on how the garment reached you.

**Scan the tag.** If a printed tag came with the garment, scan it or type its
short code at [the scan page](/scan). It resolves to the passport and offers to
claim.

**Open the claim link.** For a garment that shipped, the seller sends a
single-use link. It works once and expires 30 days after it was created.

Either way you need an account before the claim goes through. The tag or the
link is what authorises the transfer; the account is what signs it. Without that
rule anyone who scanned a rack in a shop could move the chain to themselves with
nothing on the transfer to trace it by.

Once you claim, the passport is yours. The buyer guarantee moves with it, and so
does the full history.

## Why a history beats a snapshot

A grade tells a buyer what the garment is like now. A passport tells them what it
has been like, which is a different and often more valuable thing.

**It shows wear as a curve, not a number.** Two jackets at 7.5 are not the same
purchase if one has held 7.5 for three years and the other dropped from 9.0 in
eighteen months. The second is still declining, and nothing on a single
certificate would tell you.

**It survives you.** When you resell, the record does not restart. The next
seller inherits a garment with a documented past, and a documented past is what
makes a high asking price defensible instead of optimistic.

**It is expensive to fake.** Anyone can photograph a garment well once. Nobody
can retroactively produce four years of dated, confidence-marked events, and a
buyer who has been burned before knows the difference.

**It reduces the argument after the sale.** Most condition disputes are about
what "good used condition" meant to each side. A chain of dated assessments
replaces that conversation with a record.

The practical version: grade an item when you get it, not only when you list it.
The passport is more valuable for having a beginning, and the cheapest time to
start one is the day the garment arrives.
