AutoLister turns twenty photographed, graded items into twenty drafted listings
in one run. It is the feature that changes an afternoon into a coffee break, and
it is on the higher plans because it only makes sense at volume.

## What it needs first

Items that are ready. In practice that means catalogued, photographed and
ideally graded, because the draft is built from those.

An item with no brand produces a weak title. An item with no grade produces a
description with nothing to say about condition. AutoLister is a multiplier on
the work you already did, not a substitute for it.

## What it writes

**Titles.** Brand, item, size and the distinguishing detail, in an order built
for how people search, and inside the length limit of the channel you are
targeting.

**Descriptions.** The body, including the condition summary from the grade
report and the certificate link.

**Item specifics.** Mapped from what you catalogued, with eBay's category
requirements checked, including the 65-character limit that otherwise strands a
publish.

**Prices.** From the comps, if you ran them. From your own history if you did
not.

## What it leaves you

Anything requiring judgement it does not have.

It will not invent a flaw the grade did not find, and it will not claim
authenticity. Both are deliberate. A description that overstates condition is
the exact thing grading exists to prevent, and a drafting tool that undid that
would be working against the product it sits inside.

It also leaves the final price. The suggestion is real and it is a suggestion,
and a batch of forty is exactly the situation where one systematically wrong
price is expensive.

## Why drafts, not published listings

Because a batch that published directly would turn one bad input into forty live
mistakes on a marketplace, and undoing forty live listings is considerably
harder than reviewing forty drafts.

So the run produces drafts, you skim them, and publishing is a separate step you
take deliberately. The review is fast: most drafts need nothing, and the ones
that do are usually obvious at a glance.

## How a run works

Select the items, start the batch, and it works through them. You can leave the
page; it runs on the server rather than in your browser.

Progress is per item, so you can see which are done, which are in flight and
which failed. A failure on one item does not stop the batch, because a single
bad item should not cost you the other thirty-nine.

[Reading a batch](/help/autolister/reading-a-batch) covers the statuses.

## When it is worth it

Roughly, above about ten items at a time.

Below that, the composer is fine and you keep full control for not much extra
effort. Above it, the difference compounds: twenty items drafted individually is
an evening, and twenty drafted in a batch and reviewed is half an hour.

The other case is consistency. Forty listings written by a person over three
evenings vary in structure and quality. Forty drafted in one run and edited are
uniform, which matters more than it sounds when a repeat buyer is comparing two
of your listings.

## Consistency is the underrated part

The obvious benefit is speed. The one sellers notice later is uniformity.

Forty listings written by a person across three evenings vary in structure,
detail and quality, and a buyer comparing two of yours sees that. Forty drafted
in one run and edited read as one shop.

That matters most for repeat buyers, who are the cheapest sales you will ever
make and the ones most sensitive to whether you look like a going concern.
