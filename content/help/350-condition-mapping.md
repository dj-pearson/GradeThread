Every marketplace has its own condition dropdown, they all differ, and none of
them has room for a number. Mapping is how a grade becomes something those
dropdowns accept.

## The problem

eBay offers a short list per category, and "Pre-owned" covers everything from a
9 down to a 3. Poshmark uses seller-declared tiers with no published definition
behind them. Vinted, Mercari, Grailed and Depop each have their own.

So the same garment is "Pre-owned" on one channel, "Excellent used condition" on
another, and "Very good" on a third, and none of those words tell a buyer
anything a different seller could not have written about a worse garment.

## What the mapping does

FlipDesk suggests the correct value on each channel from the grade.

An Excellent grade maps to eBay's "Pre-owned" and to the equivalent tier
elsewhere. A NWT grade maps to "New with tags" where the category offers it. The
mapping is per channel and, on eBay, per category, because the available values
differ by category.

That saves you translating by hand across four channels and, more importantly,
saves you translating inconsistently, which is what happens when a human does it
forty times.

## It is a suggestion, not a lock

You can change it, and there are legitimate reasons to.

You may know something the photos did not show. The grade assesses what it can
see, and if you know a garment has been dry cleaned or has a fault that is not
visible, your judgement is better informed than the mapping.

Some categories offer condition values that no grade maps onto cleanly. "For
parts or not working" is a statement about intent as much as condition.

And occasionally the honest answer is a step below the mapping. Nothing stops
you being more conservative than the grade, and a buyer who receives something
better than the label said does not complain.

## Where the grade actually goes

Not in the condition field, because that field is a fixed list.

The grade goes three places:

**The description.** The number, the tier, and the report's own condition
summary as the starting text.

**The certificate link.** Near the top of the description. This is the part that
does the work: a buyer clicks through to a public page with the factor breakdown
and the photos the grade was made from.

**Nowhere on the images.** Deliberately. Several marketplaces treat overlaid
graphics on listing photos as a policy problem, and a listing removed for a
watermark helps nobody. The grade is stated as text, which no channel objects
to.

## Why this is the whole point

The condition dropdown is the same four words every seller uses, and buyers have
learned to discount it accordingly.

The grade underneath is a number produced by a fixed, published rubric, with a
certificate anybody can open. It does not replace the dropdown; it gives the
dropdown something behind it.

[Marketplace condition standards](/grading/platform-standards) has the detail on
each channel's own scheme and how a grade lines up against it.

## When the marketplace has fewer options than you need

Some categories offer two condition values where the grade distinguishes seven
tiers.

In that case the mapping picks the closest and the grade does the rest of the
work, in the description and on the certificate. That is the honest arrangement:
the dropdown says what the dropdown can say, and the specific claim lives
somewhere with room for it.

It is also why the certificate link matters most on exactly those channels.

## Check it once per category

The mapping is per channel and, on eBay, per category, so the first time you
list into a category you have not used before is worth a glance at what it
picked.

After that it is consistent, which is the point. What you are checking for is a
category whose condition list is unusual, not a mistake that repeats.
