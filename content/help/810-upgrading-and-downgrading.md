The two directions behave differently, and the asymmetry is intentional rather
than an oversight.

## Upgrading takes effect immediately

Press it, and the new allowance and the new tools are there.

The charge is prorated: you pay the difference for the remainder of the current
period rather than a full period on top of what you already paid.

Upgrading mid-month is therefore not a penalty, which matters because the usual
reason people upgrade is that they have run into a limit right now.

## Downgrading takes effect at the end of the period

You keep what you bought until the period you already paid for runs out. Then
the new tier applies.

The alternative would be cutting your access the moment you clicked, which is
taking back something already paid for. The trade is that a downgrade is not an
instant way to stop being charged for the current period, and cancelling has the
same shape for the same reason.

## What happens to your work

Nothing is deleted when a tier changes. This is worth saying explicitly because
it is the fear that stops people downgrading when they should.

**Items, grades and certificates** stay. Certificates you have shared keep
working at their URLs, which matters because those links are in buyers' order
histories.

**Drafts** stay, including ones the AutoLister produced. A draft is an ordinary
listing once it exists, and the tool that helped write it becoming unavailable
does not unwrite it.

**Live listings** stay live.

**Credits** stay. They are not tied to a plan and they do not expire.

What you lose is the ability to do more of the thing: no new AutoLister batches
below the tier that includes it, a smaller monthly allowance, and so on.

## Deciding which way to move

**Upgrade** when you are hitting the allowance, or when a tool you would use
sits above your tier. The test is straightforward: if the extra allowance would
cost more bought as credits than the tier difference, the tier is cheaper before
you count the tools.

**Downgrade** when the allowance is going unused month after month. An unused
allowance is not saved, it resets, so paying for headroom you never reach is
simply paying more.

The pattern worth watching for is seasonal. If you are busy for four months and
quiet for eight, a lower tier plus credits often beats a higher tier held all
year.

## Switching between credits and a subscription

There is no switch, because they coexist. Buying credits does not cancel a plan
and starting a plan does not consume credits.

If you are moving from credits to a subscription, keep whatever credits you have.
They will absorb the overflow in busy months, and the allowance is spent first
precisely so they are not wasted.

## Changing your mind again

There is no limit and no penalty. Upgrade, downgrade, upgrade again. Each change
follows the same rules: up is immediate and prorated, down waits for the period
you already paid for.

## The seasonal pattern

Resale is seasonal for most people, and the plan that suits a busy autumn is
rarely the plan that suits a quiet spring.

The arrangement that handles that well is a lower tier held all year plus credits
bought when volume rises. Credits never expire, the allowance is spent before
them, and the total is usually less than holding a high tier through the quiet
months.

The arrangement that handles it badly is upgrading in October and forgetting to
downgrade until March.

## Set a reminder

Downgrades take effect at the end of the period, so deciding late in a period
costs you nothing and deciding after it has renewed costs a period.

One calendar reminder a few days before the renewal date is enough, and it is the
cheapest optimisation available on this page.
