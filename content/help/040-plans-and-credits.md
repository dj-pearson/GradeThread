There are three ways to pay for grading, and they suit three genuinely
different people. Picking the wrong one is not expensive, but it is annoying,
so this is the short version of who each is for.

## The free plan

Three grades a month, no card, no expiry on the account itself.

This is not a trial that turns into something. It stays free, and three a month
is a real allowance for someone selling occasionally. If you list a few things
a season, you may never need anything else.

What it does not include is FlipDesk, the reseller workflow: inventory,
crosslisting, comps, the AutoLister, payout reconciliation. Those are
subscription features because they only make sense at volume.

## Credits

Credits are a one-off purchase. You buy a pack, each grade spends one, and
unspent credits sit on the account indefinitely.

This is the right shape if your selling is lumpy. Clearing a wardrobe over one
weekend and then nothing for four months is a bad fit for a monthly charge and
a good fit for a pack of credits you work through at your own pace.

Packs get cheaper per grade as they get bigger, which is the usual trade: you
pay less per unit for committing more up front.

## A FlipDesk subscription

A monthly plan with a grade allowance included, plus the reseller tooling.

This is for people who list continuously. The maths is straightforward: if your
monthly grading would cost more as credits than the plan costs, the plan is
cheaper before you count any of the FlipDesk features, and those are where the
time saving is.

The tiers differ in allowance and in which tools unlock. AutoLister, which
drafts listings in batches, sits above the entry tier because it is the feature
that turns twenty items into an afternoon rather than a weekend.

## Which one

Rough guide, and the app will not argue if you pick differently:

- Under three grades a month: free plan.
- Bursts of selling with quiet gaps: credits.
- Steady weekly listing, or more than a handful of marketplaces: subscription.

You can hold credits and a subscription at the same time. The allowance is
spent first and credits cover the overflow, so a busy month does not stop you
working.

## Running out

Nothing breaks. An item that is waiting on a grade stays in your inventory with
everything else you have already done to it intact: photos, measurements,
draft text. The grade is the only step that pauses.

You will see the remaining allowance in the sidebar before you get near zero,
which is deliberate; a surprise at the moment you are trying to list something
is the worst possible time to find out.

## Changing your mind

Upgrades take effect immediately and are prorated. Downgrades take effect at the
end of the period you have already paid for, so you keep what you bought.

Cancelling stops the renewal. It does not delete your data, your grades or your
certificates. Certificates you have already shared stay live at their URLs,
which matters because those links are sitting in buyers' order histories and
breaking them would be a poor way to say goodbye.

Refunds follow the [refund policy](/refund), and EU and UK consumers have
statutory rights on top of it that the policy does not shorten.

## Where to look

Billing lives in the Account area. It shows the current plan, the remaining
allowance, the credit balance, and every invoice. If a payment fails you get an
email and a banner rather than a silent downgrade, and the account keeps
working through a short grace period so a declined card on a Friday does not
cost you the weekend.
