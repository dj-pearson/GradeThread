A report has five parts. This is what each one is for and which of them belong
in your listing.

## The headline

One number to one decimal place, and a tier name.

The tier is what a buyer skims and the number is what a buyer compares. Put
both in your listing: "Excellent, graded 8.2" does more work than either alone,
because the word sets expectations and the number lets somebody choose between
your jacket and the other one.

## The five factors

Each scored in half-point steps, with its weight shown:

- Fabric condition, 30 percent
- Structural integrity, 25 percent
- Cosmetic appearance, 20 percent
- Functional elements, 15 percent
- Odour and cleanliness, 10 percent

Read this as a diagnosis rather than a verdict. It tells you which part of the
garment pulled the number down, and therefore whether anything is worth doing
about it.

A low cosmetic score is often a wash away, and cosmetic is 20 percent. A low
fabric score is the garment's remaining life and is not fixable, so price
accordingly rather than trying. [The five factors](/help/grading/the-five-factors)
covers what sits in each.

## The written summary

A few sentences describing the garment's condition in words, including any
flaws it identified.

This is the part to mine for your listing description. It is already phrased as
an honest, specific account of the condition, which is exactly what a good
listing description is and exactly what most sellers find hardest to write.

If the summary mentions something you cannot see in your own photos, that is
worth attention. The model is inferring from something adjacent, and that
inference is usually why confidence came out lower than you expected. Shoot
that area properly and resubmit.

## The confidence line

How much the assessment rests on what the photos actually showed, versus
inference.

Below 0.75 the grade is held for a human reviewer rather than published. Above
it, the grade published directly. Either way the number is on the report.

High confidence with a grade you disagree with is worth a dispute. Low
confidence with a grade you disagree with is worth better photos first, because
a reviewer looking at the same unusable photos will reach the same conclusion.
See [Confidence and human review](/help/grading/confidence-and-human-review).

## The certificate

A link to a public page showing the grade, the factor breakdown and the photos
it was made from. It needs no account to open.

This is the part that does the commercial work. Put the link near the top of
your listing, not buried at the bottom. The buyer who clicks it is the buyer who
was hesitating, and the certificate is what turns your description from an
opinion into something they can check.

The page also carries a QR code, which is what makes it work on a printed tag
at a market stall.

## What to do with all of it

A workable pattern for a listing:

1. Title: brand, item, size, and the tier.
2. First line of the description: the number and the certificate link.
3. Condition section: the flaw notes from the summary, in your own words.
4. Photos: your styled listing photos, not the flat grading ones.

The grading photos are deliberately boring, because flat and even is what an
accurate assessment needs. Your listing photos are a different job and should
look like it. See
[lighting and background](/help/grading/lighting-and-background) for why the two
sets are not interchangeable.

## The revision notice

If a grade has been changed since it published, whether by a dispute or a
regrade, the report and the certificate both say so and show the current
values.

They do not silently swap the number. A buyer who screenshotted the old
certificate deserves to see that it changed, rather than to conclude they
misread it, and a seller deserves a record of which version a particular sale
was made against.

## Printing and sharing

The certificate page carries a QR code, which is what makes it work away from a
screen. Sellers at markets and fairs print it onto a tag; a buyer scans it
standing at the rail and sees the same page a web visitor would.

There is also an embeddable badge for your own site or shop, and a share card
that unfurls properly when the link is pasted into a message or a forum. Those
are covered in the certificates section.

## If the report looks wrong

Work through it in this order, because the answer is usually earlier in the
list than people assume.

1. Check the confidence line. Low confidence means better photos first.
2. Check which factor is low, not just the overall. The diagnosis is more
   actionable than the score.
3. Check the summary for a flaw you did not intend to show, or one it inferred.
4. Only then dispute it.
